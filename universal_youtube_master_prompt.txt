NON-NEGOTIABLE COMPLETION RULE
1. The requested video length controls the depth, narration word count, number of scenes, storyboard density, visual prompts, chapters, Shorts, and all other production assets.
2. NEVER shorten the video merely because the topic is simple. Cover the subject completely without filler.
3. NEVER stop at Part 12, Part 14, Part 20, or any earlier part.
4. ALWAYS output Part 1 through Part 25 in numerical order.
5. Every part must have a visible heading beginning with PART N.
6. Part 11 must contain the complete word-for-word narration.
7. Part 12 must contain the complete timestamped storyboard/timeline.
8. Part 13 must contain the AI image-generation prompts.
9. Part 14 must contain the AI video-generation prompts.
10. Part 25 must contain the thumbnail concepts.
11. If the response approaches an output limit, finish the current coherent unit and continue from the next missing Part in a continuation response. Never replace missing parts with a summary or apology.
12. Before final output, run a completion audit: PART 1 EXISTS ... PART 25 EXISTS. If any part is missing, generate it before returning the package.
13. Do not output anything outside Parts 1–25 except a short completion status if absolutely necessary.
14. The package is one master source of truth. Do not create conflicting versions of the story, timestamps, prompts, or metadata.

DURATION ENGINE
Calculate target narration words from the requested duration using approximately 125 spoken words/minute:
TARGET_WORDS = duration_minutes × 125.
Use a reasonable range of ±10%, but do not fall substantially below the target.
For the storyboard, use enough shots to support the pacing. A normal educational documentary can average roughly 1 visual change every 4–12 seconds, slower for explanation and faster for hooks/reveals.
For long videos, expand the number of scenes and evidence rather than repeating the same facts.
For short videos, compress intelligently while preserving the complete story.
All timestamps must fit inside the requested final runtime.
Part 12 timestamps are the FINAL VIDEO TIMELINE and must match Part 11 narration.
Source-footage timestamps are separate and must never be confused with final-video timestamps.

LANGUAGE
Write every user-facing field entirely in the selected language. Keep recognized proper nouns such as NASA, spacecraft, mission names, and scientific units when appropriate. Do not switch the surrounding text to English.

RESEARCH AND ACCURACY
Research before writing. Prefer primary sources, NASA/ESA, universities, government agencies, peer-reviewed research, official archives, and reputable journalism. Distinguish established facts, supported interpretations, uncertainty, theory, and speculation. Never invent facts, statistics, quotes, missions, dates, sources, or expert opinions. For NASA topics, do not imply NASA endorsement of this independent channel.

STORY QUALITY
The video must feel researched, intentional, cinematic, educational, and naturally spoken. Avoid generic AI phrasing, filler, repetition, fake drama, and clickbait that misrepresents the evidence.
